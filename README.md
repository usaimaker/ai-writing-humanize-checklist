# A practical checklist for making AI-assisted writing sound human

An open, no-signup editing checklist. This is about **editing quality**, not about fooling detectors. There is no reliable way to "beat" a detector, and this repo makes no such claim. The goal is simpler: text that a human actually wants to read.

## Why this exists

Most AI-assisted drafts fail for the same handful of reasons, and all of them are measurable. You can fix them by hand in a few minutes once you know what to look for.

## The 5 checks

### 1. Sentence-length variance

Machine-written prose tends toward uniform sentences. Measure it: split your draft into sentences, count the words in each, and look at the spread. If nearly every sentence is 14–20 words, the rhythm will feel flat no matter how good the vocabulary is. Fix it by deliberately writing one 4-word sentence after a long one, and by merging two medium sentences when the paragraph is getting choppy.

### 2. Transition and filler stacking

Phrases like "Moreover", "Furthermore", "It's worth noting that", "In today's fast-paced world", and "Delve into" rarely carry information. They are scaffolding. Delete them and re-read; if the paragraph still works, they were never doing anything. A useful rule: one transition per paragraph, maximum, and only when the relationship between the two ideas is not already obvious.

### 3. Hedging density

"May", "might", "could potentially", "generally speaking", "in many cases" — individually fine, collectively they drain a piece of authority. Count them. If a 300-word section has more than five, decide what you actually believe and state it, then keep the one hedge that is genuinely warranted.

### 4. The closing-summary tell

A final paragraph that restates the three points above, in the same order, is the most recognizable AI pattern there is. Replace it with one of: a concrete next action, a specific number, an honest limitation, or a question the reader can answer for themselves. Or just stop earlier.

### 5. Concrete detail vs. generic nouns

"Users", "businesses", "challenges", "solutions", "the landscape" are placeholders. Swap each one for something a reader can picture: the person, the tool, the date, the amount, the error message. Specificity does more for credibility than any amount of polish.

## A 3-minute pass

1. Paste the draft into a plain text editor and read only the first sentence of every paragraph. If they all sound alike, rewrite three of them.
2. Search for the filler words in check 2 and delete at least half of them.
3. Read the last paragraph. If it is a summary, cut it or replace it with something specific.

That is usually enough. Over-editing is its own failure mode — a draft that has been smoothed until every sentence is balanced reads as machine-written again.
